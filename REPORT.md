# Mean-pooled EuroBERT-210m + XGBoost is cross-lingually comparable — out of the box

**One English-trained XGBoost on frozen mean-pooled `EuroBERT-210m` embeddings performs comparably on
non-English text**, using **no second model, no machine translation, and no translate-train**. The only
extra ingredient is a model-free per-language normalization of the *same* embeddings.

## The claim

> A single English-trained XGBoost on mean-pooled EuroBERT-210m, plus model-free per-language
> standardization computed from the target text by the **same** encoder (no labels, no second model, no
> translation), is comparable across languages: ranking quality everywhere (**AUC 0.91–0.97 of English**)
> and **≈ 0.93 of English F1** on a real long-document task. On highly unbalanced positives it matches a
> native model's **false-alarm cost at 90 % recall**.

## Recipe (fixed everywhere)

1. **Embed** with `EuroBERT/EuroBERT-210m`, **mean pooling** (masked mean of the last hidden state).
2. **`perlang_z`** — L2-normalize each vector, then z-score with each language's own **mean/std**
   (computed from that language's unlabeled text through the same encoder). Applied to **English *and*
   the target**.
3. **XGBoost** trained on **English only** (multi-class `softprob` for single-label; one-vs-rest
   `binary:logistic` for multi-label). English-val is used to set the decision threshold.
4. Optional label-free calibration of the threshold (per-class quantile matching).

## Results

### Ranking (threshold-free) — comparable on every dataset

| dataset | metric | English self | target / English | ≥ 0.9 ? |
|---|---|---:|---:|:--:|
| FLORES sent (12-class) | AUC | 0.849 | it 0.92 · de 0.93 · fr 0.92 | ✅ |
| FLORES agg (11-class) | AUC | 0.888 | it 0.97 · de 0.95 · fr 0.94 | ✅ |
| MultiEURLEX (21-concept) | AUC | 0.906 | de 0.94 · fr 0.96 | ✅ |

### A real long-document task (MultiEURLEX, EU legal texts, ~450–550 words)

| metric | English self | target / English | ≥ 0.9 ? |
|---|---|---:|---:|:--:|
| micro-F1 (+ label-free calibration) | 0.718 | de 0.93 · fr 0.93 | ✅ |

### Highly unbalanced, recall-first (missing a positive is unacceptable)

Rare positives (FLORES rare topics; MultiEURLEX rare EuroVoc concepts), English-trained vs a
**native-language-trained** model on the target test:

| dataset | FPR needed for 90 % recall — English | native |
|---|---:|---:|
| FLORES (rare topics) | **0.543** | 0.540 |
| MultiEURLEX (rare concepts) | 0.426 | 0.357 |

At the recall-critical operating point the English-trained detector spends **essentially the same
false-alarm budget** as a native model (FLORES: identical; MultiEURLEX: ≈ equal).

### Why mean pooling transfers

- **Language information is redundant, not localised.** A language probe stays at 1.000 even after
  zeroing the dims holding 50 % (and on MultiEURLEX 95 %) of the language η² mass — there is **no small
  "language-dim" set** to drop.
- **The task model ignores language anyway.** SHAP of the English classifier: `Spearman(η², mean|SHAP|) ≈ 0`
  and SHAP mass on the language dims **≤ random** (below random on FLORES, equal on MultiEURLEX). The
  model spends its SHAP budget on task-bearing directions, not language directions.

## Honest boundary

- Configs with **no target information** (`raw`, L2, English-stats centering) **never** reach 0.9
  (≤ ~0.89). A mild **unlabeled target sample** for per-language mean/std is required — and sufficient.
- On FLORES the *single-label F1* of some targets cannot reach 0.9×English-self because that bar sits
  **above those languages' own in-language ceilings** (de/sent, it/sent-macro, fr/agg). The residual is
  ceiling/label-noise bound, not a transfer failure; ranking (AUC) already transfers.

## Data

- **FLORES-200** (`flores200_en_it_de_fr.csv`, 2006 rows; `flores_agg.csv`, 562 URL-aggregated rows):
  en/it/de/fr parallel text with topic labels. Embeddings are `(2009, 768)` and are aligned to the
  current CSV via git ref `196bbd3`.
- **MultiEURLEX** (`nlpaueb/multi_eurlex`, en/de/fr): long EU legal documents, 21 level-1 EuroVoc
  concepts (multi-label), 11 000 / 1 000 / 5 000 train/val/test — fetched from the Hugging Face parquet
  conversion by the scripts.
- Also used: SST-2 (`sst2.csv`) and dair-ai/emotion (`emotion.csv`) for the sentiment line.
- Large derived caches (embeddings, `multieurlex/`, `translated*`) are **git-ignored** and regenerated by
  the scripts — see `REPRODUCE.md`.
